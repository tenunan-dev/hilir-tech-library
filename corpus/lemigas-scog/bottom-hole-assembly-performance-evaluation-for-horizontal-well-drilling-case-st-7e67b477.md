ABSTRACT - Horizontal well drilling is highly complex, particularly in the landing section, due to the increased 
inclination angle, which can increase torque and drag. These conditions reduce the effectiveness of Weight -on-
Bit (WOB) transfer, lower the Rate of Penetr ation (ROP), extend drilling time, and increase the risk of hanging 
problems, which could lead to a stuck-pipe case. This study aims to evaluate the performance of the Bottom Hole 
Assembly (BHA) design in Well AM-63 and to formulate recommendations for a more effective BHA design for 
future wells. The study employs a quantitative, evaluative case study approach. The data analyzed include the 
wellbore trajectory, BHA configuration, drilling parameters, torque and drag, ROP, and drilling time in the 12%" 
section (2,310-4,195 ftMD). The analysis results show that a relatively heavy BHA configuration increases the 
contact force between the drillstring and the wellbore wall near the landing section (4.004-4.195 ft MD), resulting 
in increased torque and  drag, decreased ROP, and increased drilling time. The actual drilling time for the 124" 
section was 160 hours, which was longer than the planned 65 hours, resulting in an additional 95 hours. This study 
provides a quantitative evaluation, based on field data, of the performance of the Bottom Hole Assembly (BHA) 
at the Penang Field by demonstrating the relationship between BHA configuration, wellbore trajectory, and 
increases in torque and drag due to friction. The findings of this study serve as a practical reference for optimizing 
BHA design while highlighting the trade-off between successful drilling direction control and efforts to minimize 
friction in horizontal well drilling operations. Based on the evaluation results, it is recommended to use a lighte r 
BHA configuration to reduce torque and drag, improve WOB transfer efficiency, and minimize the risk of hanging 
while drilling subsequent horizontal wells. The results provide practical guidance for improving drilling efficiency 
and for optimizing BHAs in future horizontal well developments under similar field conditions.

1. Introduction 
Horizontal well drilling has become a widely adopted technology in oil and gas field development because it 
increases reservoir contact, thereby boosting productivity and hydrocarbon recovery. As technology advances, the 
lateral length of wells continues t o increase, and their use is becoming more widespread -both in offshore and 
onshore operations-because they enhance the efficiency of field development (Mitchell et al., 2011).  
Despite its advantages, horizontal well driling presents challenges for drilling mechanics, particularly in the 
landing section. In this interval, an increase in the inclination angle toward 90 deg increases the contact force 
between the drillstring and th e borehole wall, thereby increasing drag forces and reducing the effectiveness of 
Weight on Bit (WOB) transfer. (Al-Saedi et al., 2020). 
These conditions can lead to hanging and potentially to a stuck pipe, increasing drilling time and costs. One 
factor contributing to this situation is the design of the Bottom Hole Assembly (BHA), particularly the 
configuration and weight distribution of the drilling string. 
The well is located in the southeastern area of the Pinang field, in the vicinity of wells Pinang #54, Pinang 
#43, Pinang #52, and Pinang #36. The target formation for well P_PINA23_HW01 is the BK3460 Sand, with an 
estimated Initial Production (IP) of 340 BOPD. 
This problem occurred during drilling of Well AM -63, where a hang -up occurred upon entering the landing 
section due to increased drag forces, resulting in suboptimal WOB transfer to the bit. Therefore, this study aims 
to evaluate the BHA design and drilling mechanics conditions in Well AM-63 to identify the primary cause of the 
high drag. The evaluation results are then used as the basis for optimizing the BHA design in upcoming wells by 
adjusting the configuration and reducing the weight of BHA components (Elsayed et al., 2021). This optimization 
is expected to reduce drag forces, improve WOB transfer, and minimize the risk of hanging, thereby making 
drilling operations more efficient and economical. 
 
2. Methodology 
This study employs a quantitative analysis method using a case study approach focused on well AM-63, which 
experienced a hanging in the landing section (12 -1/4"). The data used include the well trajectory, Bottom Hole 
Assembly (BHA) configuration, drilling  parameters, torque and drag data, Weight on Bit (WOB), and Rate of 
Penetration (ROP). All operational data were verified for consistency and completeness before being used in the 
evaluation process. 
The evaluation was conducted by analyzing the relationship between BHA design, torque and drag values, and 
drilling performance. The evaluation results were used to optimize the BHA design for subsequent wells by 
adjusting component configurations to reduce drag and torque, improve WOB transfer, and minimize the risk of 
hanging. The analytical procedure consisted of three main stages. First, the well trajectory and BHA configuration 
were evaluated to identify drilling conditions that could increase mechanic al resistance in the landing section. 
Second, torque and drag simulations were performed using WellPlan software and compared with actual field 
measurements obtained from Rigsite records. Finally, the simulated and measured responses were interpreted by 
correlating changes in PickUp (P/U), Slack -Off (S/O), Torque Off Bottom, Torque On Bottom, Rate of 
Penetration (ROP), and drilling time to determine the influence of the existing BHA configuration on the 
occurrence of hanging. Torque and drag simulations wer e performed using open -hole friction factor values of 
0.30, 0.40, and 0.50, representing low, moderate, and relatively high friction conditions commonly encountered 
during directional and horizontal drilling operations. These values were selected to evaluate the sensitivity of the 
simulated hookload and torque responses under different wellbore contact conditions. The friction factor that 
produced the closest agreement between simulation results and field measurements was considered to represent 
best the mechanical conditions encountered during drilling of the landing section.

2.1 Well plan dan well trajectory 
A well plan is a drilling path designed to guide drilling operations so that the target reservoir can be reached 
safely and efficiently. The drilling path for Well AM-63 was designed using the "build and hold" profile method 
until an inclination angle approaching horizontal was reached in the reservoir interval. Figure 1 shows the wellbore 
design for Well AM-63, indicating that the landing section is the most complex part because the sharp change in 
inclination angle increases contact forces between the drilling string and the wellbore wall. According to drilling 
mechanics principles, the increase in inclination angle also increases the normal contact force acting between the 
drill string and the wellbore wall. This increased contact force leads to greater frictional resistance, resulting in 
higher torque and drag as the drilling operation approaches the horizontal section. These conditions can increase 
torque and drag, thereby contributing to hanging problems during drilling.

• Comparison of the well plan and the actual well path 
A comparison between the planned trajectory (well plan) and the actual trajectory was conducted to 
determine the success rate of directional control during the operation. Based on the well plot in Figure 2, the 
actual trajectory of Well AM-63 generally follows the planned trajectory from the vertical phase through the 
build and landing sections. The results of the latest survey (Last Survey) show a Center-to-Center (CC) value 
of 6.9 ft at the end of the 12 -1/4" section. These deviations are relatively small, indicating that the drilling 
process was able to keep the drill  path within the target. These results indicate that the directional drilling 
operation successfully achieved the planned well trajectory; therefore, trajectory deviation was not considered 
the primary cause of the hanging condition observed in Well  AM-63. Thus, the landing was successfully 
achieved as designed, and the deviation from the plan was not the cause of the hanging problem. This problem 
is more closely related to the mechanics of drilling, specifically to increases in torque and drag and decreases 
in the effectiveness of Weight on Bit (WOB) transfer as the inclination angle approaches the horizontal.

2.2 Design of bottom hole assembly (BHA) well AM-63 
The Bottom Hole Assembly (BHA) used in the drilling of the AM-63 Landing Section (12-1/4") consists of a 
12-1/4" bit, 8" Downhole Motor (DHM), 8" float sub, 8" M/LWD (Gamma Ray/Resistivity) sub, 8" crossover 
sub, 6-1/2" jar, and a combination of HWDP and D C designed to support horizontal drilling operations until the 
landing section is reached.  
Based on Table 1, the BHA AM-63 uses several large-diameter components that are relatively heavy, including 
the 8" DHM, 8" Float Sub, 8" M/LWD, and 8" Cross  Over Sub. These components provide sufficient stiffness 
and directional control during drilling, however, their relatively large diameter and weight also increase the normal 
contact force between the drillstring and the wellbore wall, particularly in the landing section. Consequently, this 
configuration contributes to higher torque and drag values, reducing Weight on Bit (WOB) transfer efficiency 
during drilling. 
2.3 Analysis of drilling parameters 
• Analysis of rate of penetration (ROP) & drilling time 
The Rate of Penetration (ROP) evaluation focused on the 12% -inch borehole interval, particularly as 
drilling entered the landing section. The analysis uses data from the Daily Drilling Report (DDR), which 
includes drilling depth, Weight on Bit (WOB), RPM, flow rate, drilling torque, and drilling time. This data is 
used to evaluate changes in ROP values during drilling  operations and their relationship to increases in 
operations and their relationship to increases in torque and drag. Based on Table 2, the ROP during sliding 
decreased in the interval around 3,640-4,170 ft MD compared to the previous interval, even though the WOB 
and flow rate remained relatively constant. This indicates that the decrease in ROP is influenced not only by 
drilling parameters but also by increased mechanical resistance in the form of torque and drag, which reduces 
the effectiveness of transferring the Weight on Bit (WOB) to the drill bit.

• Torque and drag analysis 
Torque and drag analyses were conducted to evaluate the drilling mechanics in the 12¼ - inch section of 
Well AM-63. These two parameters were used to determine the magnitude of the frictional force between 
the drillstring and the borehole wall. An increase in drag can hinder the transfer of Weight on Bit (WOB) 
to the drill bit, while an increase in torque indicates increased rotational resistance due to contact between 
the drill string and the formation. These conditions can reduce drilling efficiency and ca use hanging 
problems in the landing section. The actual hookload and torque data in Table 4 were used as the basis for 
the analysis and then presented in the form of a roadmap Figure to facilitate interpretation of changes in 
each parameter along the drilling .

3. Result and discussion 
3.1 Evaluation of the BHA design on the drilling performance of well AM-63 
Based on the data analysis, it was found that during the drilling of the 12%" section of Well AM-63, there was 
an increase in torque and drag values, followed by a decrease in the Rate of Penetration (ROP) and an increase in 
drilling time during the landing section interval. This phenomenon indicates that drilling mechanics performance 
was not optimal.  
The operational data consistently show that the onset of the hanging condition coincided with the transition 
from the build section to the landing section, where the well inclination increased significantly. Therefore, the 
discussion focuses on the relatio nship between the BHA configuration, torque and drag behavior, and drilling 
performance, as these parameters represent the principal findings of this study. 
3.2 Evaluation of the bottom hole assembly (BHA) configuration 
The AM-63 BHA uses several large -diameter components that are relatively heavy, such as an 8" DHM, an 
8" Float Sub, an 8" M/LWD, and an 8" CrossOver Sub. This configuration results in a high total BHA weight, 
which increases the normal force between the dr illstring and the borehole wall in the landing section. As the 
inclination angle increases, part of the BHA's weight is converted into contact force against the borehole wall, 
thereby increasing drag and reducing the effectiveness of Weight on Bit (WOB) transfer. As a result, the Rate of 
Penetration (ROP) decreases, and a higher WOB is required to maintain the ROP. This condition was one of the 
factors contributing to the hanging problem in Well AM63. 
The weight of the string increases the normal force on the wellbore wall, which in turn increases the friction 
force, resulting in higher torque and drag, especially on the landing section. Therefore, the BHA configuration of 
Well AM-63 is believed to be one of the factors contributing to the increase in friction force during the drilling of 
the landing section. 
According to the principles of drilling mechanics, the normal contact force between the drillstring and the 
wellbore wall increases as the inclination angle approaches the horizontal section. A heavier, stiffer BHA further 
increases this contact force, res ulting in greater frictional resistance, torque, and drag. Consequently, a smaller 
portion of the applied surface weight is transferred to the drill bit, reducing drilling efficiency. These findings are 
consistent with previous studies reported by Mitchell et al. (2011) , which explain that drillstring weight, well 
trajectory, and friction coefficient are the dominant factors controlling torque and drag behavior in highly deviated 
and horizontal wells. 
 
3.3 The relationship between BHA design and increases in torque and drag 
• Pick up (P/U) analysis 
Based on Figure 2, at depths of approximately 3,300 -3900 ft MD, the actual hookload curve begins to 
deviate from the calculated curve, particularly as it enters the build section through the landing section. The 
greatest deviation occurs toward the end of drilling, when the actual hookload lies to the right of the predicted 
curve. This condition indicates an increase in drag force due to increased friction between the drillstring and 
the borehole wall as the inclination angle reaches horizontal. This increase in drag reduce d the effectiveness 
of force transfer along the drilling string and caused the drillstring to hang in Well AM-63. 
Figure 2 clearly shows that the deviation between the predicted and actual Pick -Up hookload increases 
progressively after approximately 3,300 ft MD. This deviation indicates that frictional resistance increased 
with increasing well inclination, confirming that drag accumulation was concentrated in the landing section.

• Slack off (S/O) analysis 
Based on Figure 3, the actual curve deviates from the theoretical curve in the interval from the build section 
to the landing section, starting at a depth of 3,400-3,900 ft MD. This difference indicates that as the drill string 
is lowered, drag increases, reducing the load transmitted to the lower part of the string. Although the drill 
string can still be lowered to reach the target depth, the increase d drag results in less optimal Weight on Bit 
(WOB) transfer, thereby reducing drilling efficiency. This phenomenon is an early indication that high friction 
forces are developing, which will eventually lead to a hanging problem in the landing section.  
The trend observed in Figure 3 complements the Pick-Up analysis by demonstrating that the increased drag 
affected both pulling and lowering operations. The consistent deviation between the actual and theoretical 
curves confirms that friction was no longer localized but influenced the o verall movement of the drillstring. 
The increasing separation between the calculated and measured Slack-Off curves confirms that additional drag 
developed during downward movement of the drillstring. This behavior indicates that a significant portion of 
the applied load was dissipated through friction before reaching the drill bit, thereby reducing the efficiency 
of Weight-on-Bit (WOB) transfer. 
• Torque off bottom analysis 
Figure 4 shows that the actual curve remains between the predicted curves up to approximately 3,900 ft 
MD. After entering the landing section, the actual torque value increases and approaches or even exceeds the 
calculations based on a high friction factor. This i ndicates an increase in rotational resistance resulting from 
increased contact area between the drillistring and the wellbore. The higher the required torque, the more 
rotational energy is being used to overcome friction rather than being transferred to the bit. 
This condition is one of the causes of decreased drilling efficiency in the landing section. Figure 4 indicates 
that rotational resistance remained relatively stable in the build section but increased considerably after 
entering the landing section. This trend demonstrates that additional rotational energy was required to 
overcome friction rather than being transmitted efficiently through the drillstring. 
• Torque on bottom analysis 
Figure 5 shows that, during the landing section, the actual torque curve exhibits a fairly significant increase 
compared to the calculated results. This increase indicates that, in addition to high friction along the drill string, 
the bit also requires more energy  to maintain the drilling speed. Due to the simultaneous increase in torque 
and drag, the transfer of Weight on Bit (WOB) became less effective. This condition is consistent with the 
operational report, which documented a weight transfer issue during the landing section interval. Thus, it can 
be concluded that the increase in torque and drag was the primary factor causing the hanging problem in Well 
AM-63; therefore, an evaluation of the Bottom Hole Assembly (BHA) design is necessary as the basis for 
improvements to be implemented in subsequent wells.  
Among all torque and drag parameters, Figure 5 provides the clearest evidence of drilling inefficiency 
because it represents the drilling condition while the bit was actively cutting the formation. The significant 
increase in actual torque relative to predicted values indicates that mechanical energy was increasingly 
consumed by friction, thereby reducing the effective transfer of Weight on Bit (WOB) to the bit.

3.4 The effects of torque and drag on the decrease in ROP 
The combined interpretation of Table 3  and Figures 1  through 5 indicates that the reduction in drilling 
performance resulted from the interaction between BHA configuration, increased torque and drag, reduced Weight 
on Bit (WOB) transfer, and lower Rate of Penetration (ROP), rather than from a single operational facto r. The 
relatively heavy configuration of the Bottom Hole Assembly causes the normal force between the drillstring and 
the wellbore wall to increase upon entering the landing section. This increase in normal force results in higher 
torque and drag, preventing optimal transfer of Weight on Bit (WOB) to the drill bit. As a result of the reduced 
WOB received by the bit, the Rate of Penetration decreases, causing the drilling process to proceed more slowly. 
This situation is reflected in the increase in drilling time compared to the operational target. In addition, increased 
drag increases the pulling and thrust forces required to move the drill string, so the drill string tends to experience 
resistance to movement (hanging) in the landing section. 
Overall, the results indicate that the reduction in drilling performance was not due to insufficient drilling 
parameters but to inefficient mechanical energy transfer caused by excessive friction. Consequently, improving 
BHA design is expected to provide g reater benefits than merely increasing Weight on Bit or rotary speed under 
similar drilling conditions. 
3.5 Recommendations for design improvements to the BHA for the next well 
Based on the results of the evaluation of Well AM -63, the Bottom Hole Assembly configuration needs to be 
modified to mitigate the hanging problem in the next well. The improvements focused on reducing the weight, 
size, and stiffness of the drilling string while maintaining the ability to steer along the planned trajectory. Reducing 
the weight, size, and stiffness of the BHA is expected to lower the normal force acting on the wellbore wall, 
thereby reducing torque and drag. As friction decreases, the transfer of weight on bit to the drill bit becomes more 
effective, thereby increasing the rate of penetration and reducing drilling time.

4. Conclusion 
The impact of increased torque and drag is reflected in a longer drilling time, from the target of 64.65 hours 
to 160 hours, an increase of approximately 95 hours. This indicates that the effectiveness of Weight on Bit (WOB) 
transfer is not yet optimum in the landing section. The Bottom Hole Assembly (BHA) design for Well AM -63 
has not delivered optimal performance in the landing section. Although the actual trajectory successfully followed 
the well plan with a final deviation of only 6.9 ft center-to-center (C-C), there was a decrease in ROP in the 3833-
4195 ft MD interval, followed by an increase in torque and drag, which triggered a hanging problem that ultimately 
led to a decrease in ROP. Based on the evaluation results, improvements to the Bottom Hole A ssembly (BHA) 
design by adjusting the BHA size from 8" to 6 -3/4" were implemented as a mitigation measure for subsequent 
wells to reduce torque and drag and minimize the potential for hanging issues.
