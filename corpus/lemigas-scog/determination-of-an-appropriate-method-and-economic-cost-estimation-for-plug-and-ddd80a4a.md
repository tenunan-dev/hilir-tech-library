ABSTRACT - In accordance with applicable regulations in Indonesia, oil and gas wells that are nearing the end 
of their well life cycle and are no longer economically viable for production must undergo plug and abandon 
(P&A) operations, with the costs of such operations borne by the operating company. Hence, the operating 
company needs to conduct a study to ensure that P&A operations can be carried out cost effectively while still  
complying with safety guidelines, operating standards, and applicable national regulations. This study discusses 
a comparison of appropriate methods for plug and abandonment (P&A) activities in directional wells and 
examines the technically and economical ly of the SNI 13 -6910-2002 and NORSOK Standard D -010 methods. 
The Tambun field consists of several production wells, including the H-11 well, which is a directional well. This 
well serves as a reference for determining the implementation of plug and abandonment activities once the well’s 
condition reaches its economic limit. Plug and abandonment (P&A) activities at the Tambun field are carried out 
by designing a work plan to determine the zone sections to be cemented by calculating the volume of cement, 
additives, and completion fluids using the rig method. The results of this study are to determine the appropriate 
method and estimated costs that need to be prepared to carry out well abandonment activities in accordance with 
the year of well abandon using the SNI 13-6910-2002 and NORSOK Standard D-010 methods. The cost estimate 
for H-11 well using SNI 13-6910-2002 is 505,737.59 USD, while for NORSOK Standard D-010 it is 1,170,558.11 
USD. Therefore, based on the comprehensive technical evaluation, operational safety, regulatory compliance, and 
economic evaluation, the SNI 13-6910-2002 method is recommended for plug and abandonment of the H-11 well 
in Tambun field. 
Keywords: plug and abandonment, cost estimation, cement plug, SNI 13-6910-2002, NORSOK Standard D-010.

1. Introduction 
The upstream oil and gas industry is a capital-intensive sector that relies on advanced technology to minimize 
operational risks. One of the major challenges associated with oil and gas operations is their potential 
environmental impact. Exploration and pr oduction activities may directly or indirectly alter environmental 
conditions (Abrahamsen & Selvik 2020;  Afrisca & Darmawan 2020) . Consequently, governments, operating 
companies, and other stakeholders are responsible for protecting and managing the environment to prevent 
pollution, mitigate environmental degradation and maintain ecosystem sustainability (Herndon & Smith 1976;  
Abrahamsen & Selvik 2020). 
Production sharing contract (PSC) contractors are required to decommission production facilities and 
supporting infrastructure by permanently disabling their operational capability, including performing perman ent 
plug and abandonment (P&A) of wells. The primary objective of P&A is to isolate hydrocarbon -bearing 
formations and prevent fluid migration from the reservoir to the surface, thereby ensuring environmental 
protection and public safety.  In addition, ope rators are required to restore the affected site and submit an 
Abandonment and Site Restoration (ASR) reserve fund report for each field, including the estimated costs of well 
abandonment as part of ASR budget nomination (SKK Migas 2023).  
Well abandoned activities or commonly known as plug and abandonment (P&A) refers to the permanent closure 
of wells that are no longer economically viable for hydrocarbon production (Abrahamsen & Selvik 2020; Afrisca & 
Darmawan 2020). This process involves installing permanent barriers capable of containing formation pressure and 
preventing hydrocarbons or formation fluids from migrating to surface (Herndon & Smith 1976). Based on Work 
Procedure Guidelines of SKK Migas No. 040/PTK/SKKIA0000/2023/S9Rev.02, P&A is defined as the permanent 
plugging of well that are no longer required for production, have no further development potential, or have been 
classified as dry holes (SKK Migas 2023).  
Permanent well abandoned may be required for several reasons, including severe formation problems, 
irretrievable downhole equipment (fish), or the attainment of well’s economic limit,  beyond which continued 
production is no longer profitable (Kaiser & Liu 2014; Corina 2020). The oil and gas production of a well naturally 
declines over time until it reaches an economic threshol d established by the operating company, after which 
continued production is considered uneconomic (Prasetya 2018; Corina 2020).  
Kaiser & Dodson (2007)  and Kaiser & Liu (2014)  classified well status into several categories, including 
drilling, completed, temporary shut -in, temporarily abandoned, and permanently abandoned. At the end of the 
production contract, wells are permanently abandoned under one or more of the following conditions: 
• Hydrocarbons are discovered in marginal quantities that do not justify commercial production, resulting in 
immediate abandonment after drilling. 
• Production declines over time until it falls below the established economic limit. 
• Irreparable well integrity failures or leaks pose unacceptable operational or environmental risks. 
• The production contract expires, and no future field redevelopment is planned. 
Given the potential signatures, permanent well abandoned should be implemented in accordance with 
applicable regulations to ensure environmental protection and sustainable resource management (Lukmana 
2019). The Indonesian National Standard SNI 13-6910- 2002 states that “all wells should be abandoned in such 
a way as to ensure belowground isolation of hydrocarbons and to maintain fluid movement from the formation 
into the wellbore”.  
Effective plug and abandonment (P&A) is esse ntial for maintaining long -term well integrity by preventing 
fluid migration between geological formation and the surface, thereby reducing  environmental risks throughout 
the well life cycle (Darmawan 2021). Recent studies indicate that integrating well integrity evaluations into field 
development planning and ageing-well management improves operational reliability while reducing uncertainty 
in engineering decision-making (Prayitno et al., 2026 ). Accordingly, an effective P&A design should provide 
reliable well barriers while complying with applicable technical and environmental standards.

Permanent well abandonment in Indonesia is regulated in SNI -13-910-2002, which was established by the 
National Standardization Agency (2002). The standard divides permanent abandonment into two principal stages: 
reservoir abandonment and surface plugging. Kaiser (2017) further described the overall abandonment process as 
consisting of planning, obtaining regulatory approvals, conducting permanent well abandoned operations, 
removing the wellhead and completing final reporting. Well abandoned activities or commonly known as Plug 
and Abandonment (P&A) are activities carried out when the condition of the well is considered uneconomical to 
produce. The activity carried out to stop the well is to apply pressure to the well so that the formation pressure 
does not burst to the surface. Technology Subgroup (2011)  states that w ell abandoned is necessary to create 
barriers that prevent hydrocarbon leakage from the formation into the wellbore or to the mudline or surface and 
prevent pollution of water sources aroun d the well environment. Several cement placement techniques are 
commonly used during P&A operations, including the balanced plug, dump bailer, and two -plug methods 
(Suhascaryo & Anugrah 2023; and Suranta et al., 2026). 
The Tambun field is one of oil and gas producing fields located in the Northern part of the West Java working 
area, with sandstone and limestone as the primary reservoir formations. The H-11 well was selected as the case 
study for planning permanent abandonment and estimating the associated costs required to fu lfil abandonment 
and site restoration (ASR) obligations. Production data indicate that the H -11 well no longer contains 
economically prospective zones for further development. Consequently, the well has reached the end of its 
productive life and should be permanently abandoned, particularly because it is located near a densely populated 
area. The main objective of this study is to compare two methods that could be used to carry out permanent well 
abandonment in accordance with national and international sta ndards at H-11 well based on SNI 13 -6910-2002 
and NORSOK Standard D-010 to achieve the most efficient and cost-effectiveness results. 
The study first applied the National Standard SNI 13-6910-2002 method in planning the plug and abandonment 
process, as this standard specifies the required cement length intervals for sealing the well in the perforation zone, 
open holes, casing stubs, the annulus, surface plugs, plug testing, fluids to be left in the wellbore, and site cleanup. 
Second, based on NORSOK Standard D -010, the regulation includes provisions for primary well barriers to 
prevent fluid flow into the wellbore or to the surface. Furthermore, a secondary well barrier serves as an additional 
plug to assist the primary well barrier in blocking fluid flow, while an environmental isolation plug is designed to 
ensure the safety of the borehole within the casing and the annulus. The method used to install the cement plug is 
the balance plug method. This  method was chosen due its common use in plug -and-abandonment operations. It 
does not require specialized equipment that could increase P&A costs, and is easier to implement than other 
methods. SNI 13 -6910-2002 and NORSOK Standard D-010 both provide guidance for plug and abandonment 
planning. However, limited comparisons are available for their technical and economic performance of Indonesian 
mature onshore wells. This comparison is particularly relevant for the H-11 well since the selected abandonment 
strategy should not only comply with Indonesian regulations but also ensure adequate well integrity, operational 
safety, and cost -effectiveness. Therefore, the assessment of both standards gives a practical basis to select the 
most suitable abandonment method for mature wells under the Indonesian operating conditions.

2.2 SNI 13-6910-2002 method 
2.2.A Open-hole isolation 
Isolation zone in open holes, cement plugs should be placed at least 100 ft below the layer up to 100 ft 
above the layer containing oil, gas or water to isolate the fluids in the layer where they are encountered 
and prevent flow to other layers or to the surface. Placement of additional cement plugs is required to 
prevent potential fluids from migrating into the wellbore. Cement plugs should be installed in the 
deepest casing by the plunger method at least 100 ft below the casing shoe to 100 ft above the casing 
shoe, if there is an open hole below the casing. Alternative methods of cement plugs along the casing 
shoe are as follows: 
• Cement retainer with cement plug. The cement retainers should provide effective back -pressure 
control and be installed between 50 ft and 100 ft above the casing shoe. Meanwhile, the cement 
plug should be installed at least 100 ft below the casing shoe and to 50 ft below the retainer. 
• Permanent bridge plug. A permanent bridge plug should be installed 150 ft above the casing shoe 
with a minimum 50 -ft cement plug placed above the bridge plug. This method is recommended 
when circulation losses are anticipated or encountered. The bridge plug should be tested in 
accordance with applicable regulations. 
2.2.B Isolating of perforated intervals 
Cement plug i nstallation shall be carried out by pushing through all uncemented perforations. The 
cement plug press shall extend at least 100 ft above the perforation interval to 100 ft below the 
perforation interval or to the nearest casing plug. Acceptable methods to  replace preference and 
placement of cement plugs are: 
• Installation of cement retainer and cement plug are both mandatory. Cement retainers shall be 
capable of effective back pressure control and shall be installed at least 50 ft and a maximum of 
100 ft ab ove the perforation interval. While cement plugs must be able to reach a minimum of 
100 ft below the perforation interval and 50 ft above the retainer. 
• The permanent-type bridge plug shall be installed at 150 ft above the crest and the perforation 
interval at least 50 ft above the bridge plug. 
• Installation of cement plugs at least 200 ft long using the plunger method with the base of the plug 
at a distance of 100 ft above the top of the perforation interval. 
2.2.C Casing stump plugging 
The methods used when the casing is cut and removed, the casing stump must be plugged with: 
• Stumps within the casing string must be plugged with cement plugs a minimum of 100 ft above 
and 100 ft below the stump. Alternatives used to replace the cement plug covering the stump, as 
follows: cement retainer or permanent type bridge plug should be installed at least 50 ft above the 
stump with at least 50 ft of cement; and cement plugs that are at least 200 ft long shall be installed 
with the base of the plug at least 100 ft above the stump. 
• Zone plugging or open hole isolation shall be implemented as applicable, if the stump is 1 
2.2.D Annular isolation 
Any annular space connected to an open hole extending to the mud line shall contain a cement barrier 
with a minimum length of 200 ft. 
2.2.E Surface plugs 
A surface cement plug shall have a minimum length of 150 ft and be installed with its top located 150 
ft below the mud line. The smallest casing string extending to the surface shall also be filled with cement. 
2.2.F Plug verification 
After plug placement, verification shall be performed as follows: 
• The operator has an obligation to perform a load test with a minimum weight of 15,000 pounds 
on the cement retainer plug, or bridge plug. If the cement is above the separation plug or retainer, 
no test is required.

2.2.G Fluid left in the well 
Each consistent interval of the hole between the various cement plugs should be filled with fluid of

All wellheads, casing strings, piles and associated structures shall be removed to a depth of at least

wellheads or other obstructions do not pose a nuisance to other persons using the seabed or authorized 
users of the area, the need to r emove them to protect the site may be limited or eliminated. 
Government environmental regulations must be realized to restore the area to its original condition. 
2.3 NORSOK standard D-010 method 
According to NORSOK Standard D-010, permanently abandoned wells should be periodically monitored 
to evaluate the long-term performance of well barriers and their potential interaction with geological and chemical 
processes. Well barriers are designed to prevent fluid leakage and minimise risks during d rilling, production, 
workover, and abandonment operations (NORSOK Standard 2013; Achang et al. 2020). 
The standard classifies well barriers into two categories: 
• Primary well barrier. This is a barrier located adjacent to hydrocarbons. Assuming the primary well barrier 
is functioning effectively, it has the capacity to limit hydrocarbons that are pressurization. If the primary 
well barrier is not functioning, this is due to a leak or a valve that fails to close. 
• Secondary well barrier. A barrier that serves to block the flow of pressurized hydrocarbons out of the well. 
If there is a failure of the secondary well barriers, there is the potential for the tertiary barrier to effectively 
block the flow of hydrocarbons.  
A general description of the two categories of well barriers can be understood as, in the process of stopping the flow 
of oil wells, hydrostatic pressure serves as the primary barrier, while surface equipment such as blow out preventer 
(BOP) and casing cement serve as secondary barriers. Having at least one effective well barrier between the surface and 
the potential flow source is essential, except for subsurface formations with hydrocarbons and/or potential flow that 
require two well barriers. When performing plugging activities in reservoirs, it is important to consider the potential that 
can occur in connection with this part of the well (if the formation collapses, etc.) and to be able to create certain well 
barrier elements. Every complete borehole must be isolated. Whether or not there is pressure or flow potential, the last 
open hole section of a borehole cannot be permanently abandoned without installing a permanent well barrier.  
The permanent well barrier will extend across the entire well section, including all annuli and act as a barrier, 
both vertically and horizontally. Because of this, well barrier elements placed in the casing and as part of the 
permanent well barrier, should be placed at depth intervals where there are well barrier elements of validated quality 
in all annulus. The properties a permanent well barrier needs to have are impermeable, long -term integrity, non-
shrinking, ductile (not brittle), able to withstand mechanical/impact loads, resistance to various chemicals/substances 
(H2S, CO2 and hydrocarbons), and discussion, to ensure bonding with cost.

2.4.A Qualitative analysis using decline curve analysis (DCA), trial-and-error, and chi-square methods 
Decline curve analysis (DCA) is one of the methods that can be used to calculate the reserve estimate of 
a well. Calculations with this method are more commonly used because it only requires historical 
production data. The decline in production value obser ved in the production history data is a depiction 
of the reservoir productivity of a well without the problem of decreased production caused by several 
external factors, which reservoir conditions must be stable to be able to extrapolate the decline in the  
form of a curve (Arps 1944; Rukmana et al., 2018). According to Arps (1944) and Rukmana et al. (2018). 
The DCA is divided into three types, namely exponential, hyperbolic, and harmonic. The value to 
determine the type of decline used is commonly known as "b". The value of b only ranges from 0 to 1. If 
the value of b=0 then the curve type is exponential. If the value of 0≤b≤1 then the type of curve is a 
hyperbolic curve. Meanwhile, if the value of b = 1 then the type of curve is harmonic. The type of decline 
curve is determined before the calculation of the estimated reserves of a reservoir. The determination of 
the type of decline curve used in the research is the trial error and x 2 Chisquare-test method because it 
has more concrete results.

3. Result and discussion 
3.1 Data preparation and DCA result 
The data used in this study were obtained from H-11 well in the Tambun field located in Northern West Java. 
The available data included the well schematics, tubing and casing specifications, well perforation depth, cementing 
records (Figure 2), and historical production data. These datasets were used to design the plug and abandonment 
(P&A) program, including the calculation of cement, additive, and completion fluid requirements, work procedures, 
operational duration, and cost estimation. The P&A planning for the H-11 well was carried out in accordance with 
SNI 13-6910-2002 and NORSOK Standard D-010 methods. Based on the historical production data, a Decline curve 
analysis (DCA) was performed with a 40-year production forecast. The predicted production performance of the H-
11 well is presented in Figure 3. 
3.2 Plug and abandonment (P&A) planning 
The H-11 well was directionally drilled to a final depth of 873 3.59 ftMD. The casing installation stage began 
with hammering the 30" casing conductor to a depth of 0 - 118.1 ftMD. Next, a hole was drilled using a 26" bit, and

Best Practices: Best practices were identified through a comprehensive literature review of published field case 
studies on plug and abandonment operations. The review indicates that regulatory requirements should be 
incorporated into the early planning stage to ensure technical feasibility, operational safety, environmental protection, 
and regulatory compliance. Ultimately, the operator is responsible for selecting the most appropriate abandonment 
strategy based on well conditions, safety considerations, environmental risks, and economic performance. 
Cement Design:  Cement plugs should be designed according to the anticipated bottom -hole pressure and 
temperature at each plug location. Bottom -hole pressure primarily determines slurry density, whereas bottom -
hole temperature governs the selection and concentration of cement additives to ensure adequate pumpability and 
compressive strength development. For the H-11 well, API Class G cement was selected together with silica flour, 
liquid dispersant, liquid retarder, fluid -loss-control additive, bonding agent, and defoamer. The additive 
concentration varies for each cement plug according to the downhole conditions and design requirements. 
Cement Placement: Cement should be placed under controlled and well -conditioned wellbore conditions. In 
this study, the balanced plug method was selected because it is widely used in plug and abandonment operations, 
requires relatively simple equipment, and provides a cost -effective cement placement technique. The method 
involves lowering a drill pipe to the planned plug depth before pumping cement into the wellbore. A pre-flush is 
pumped ahead of the cement slurry to minimize mud contamination, followed by a spacer fluid to separate the 
drilling fluid from the cement slurry and improve cement placement quality.

3.1 Cement plug configuration 
Cement plugs are positioned in the perforation interval isolation zone, top isolation of the liner, and surface 
insulation in accordance with SNI 13 -6910-2002 as shown in Figure 4 and Table 1. According to the SNI 13 -
6910-2002 method, the plugging of the perforation zone intervals requires cement placement with a minimum 
distance of 100 ft above and below the perforation zone intervals, and a single cement plug is installed. Since the 
distance between the top of the liner and the perforation zone in both wells is close, a combined calculation was 
performed by integrating the two requirements for the perforation zone with the top -of-liner plugging, where the 
cement plug must extend at least 100 ft above and 100 ft below the perforation zone. A surface plug is installed 
at a depth of 0 - 150 ft; therefore, based on this method, the cement plug must be at least 150 ft long. 
The existence of an over pressured zone is n ot taken into consideration when placing the cement plug. 
Therefore, if an issue arises during the P&A work, mitigation is required. It is not necessary to insert a cement 
plug for every component of the casing shoe section, provided that the cement plug pressure can sustain the well 
formation pressure. Since there are no additional hydrocarbon zones surrounding the well and the hydrocarbon 
zone is thought to be the only one, the isolation of the hydrocarbon-bearing zone is disregarded. 
Meanwhile, with reference to NORSOK Standard D -010 method in Figure 5 and Table 2, cement plugs are 
positioned in the interval isolation zone, top of liner isolation, hydrocarbon bearing isolation zone as a secondary 
well barrier, isolation for each section of casing shoe, and isolation of the surface water zone (fresh water). It is 
necessary to place a cement plug across the transition interval, where the plug length is at least 200 feet or at least 
reaches normal pressure in certain casing sections, in order to account for the existence of an over pressured zone 
or abnormal pressure. Cementing must be done in each segment of the casing shoe while taking into consideration 
variations in the rock lithology of each formation. Considering a zone that contains other hydrocarbon intervals

3.1 Economic analysis 
Based on calculations in 2026 using the SNI 13 -6910-2002 method, the estimated cost for the H -11 well is 
USD 179,276.30. Meanwhile, using the NORSOK Standard D -010 method, the cost is USD 414,945.09. As for 
the estimated costs required based on calculations in 2066 for the H-11 well using the SNI 13-6910-2002 method, 
the cost is 505,737.59 USD, and the NORSOK Standard D-010 method is 1,170,558.11 USD. A summary of the 
estimated P&A well costs is shown in Table 5. The dominant contributor is the rig cost which is USD 158,589.18 
in the NORSOK Standard D-010 method against USD 78,738.06 in the SNI 13-6910-2002 method. Likewise, the 
cement plug unit cost rises from USD 43,090.60 to USD 157,122.00 because of the seven cement plug section s 
used by the NORSOK method instead of three. The additional plug sections also increase the cement slurry 
volume from 109.35 bbl to 160.11 bbl and extend the operational duration from 9.8 to 18 days, which results in a 
significantly higher total abandonment cost. 
Factors affecting the total estimated cost of plug and abandonment (P&A) activities are the use of both SNI 
13-6910-2002 and NORSOK Standard D-010 methods, the difference in well depth, the number of plug sections, 
the length of the cement area, t he volume of cement required, the volume of additives used, and the volume of 
completion fluid added, as well as the cost of rig rental.  
The results show that the higher abandonment cost of the NORSOK Standard D -010 approach is mainly due 
to its more cons ervative well barrier design requiring additional cement plug sections and longer rig time. This 
method does increase the operational costs, but provides for an additional layer of redundancy to ensure well 
integrity and operational safety. Meanwhile, SNI 13-6910-2002 method required the less cement plug section, but 
still satisfy the technical requirements for well isolation based on Indonesian standards. Therefore, for the H -11 
well in Tambun field, the SNI method represents the most cost -effective abando nment strategy without 
compromising regulatory compliance or operational safety.  
 
4. Conclusion 
Based on the results of the analysis and study that has been carried out, the H -11 well is expected to reach its 
economic limit and be permanently abandoned in 2066. The proposed plug and abandonment operation employs 
the balanced plug cementing method  using a conventional  550-HP drilling rig  with 3½-in drill pipe  for 
the 8,733.59-ftMD well. From a technical perspective, the  SNI 13 -6910-2002 design requires an esti mated 
operational duration of  9.8 days , whereas the  NORSOK Standard D -010 design requires approximately  18 
days because of the installation and verification of additional well barriers. Economic analysis indicates that the 
projected abandonment cost in  2066 is USD 505,737.59  using SNI 13 -6910-2002, compared with  USD 
1,170,558.11 using NORSOK Standard D -010. Considering the technical assessment, operational duration,
