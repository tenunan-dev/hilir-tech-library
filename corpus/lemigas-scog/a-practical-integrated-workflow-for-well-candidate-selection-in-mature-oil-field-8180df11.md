ABSTRACT - Mature oil fields commonly face production decline, increasing water cut, and limited availability 
of complete static and dynamic reservoir models. Under these conditions, a practical and traceable workflow is 
necessary to select well candidates for produc tion optimization. This study proposes an integrated workflow 
combining well opportunity index (WOI), Delta Potential, quadrant analysis, and weighted ranking. The workflow 
is applied to three producing wells in the G-40 sandstone reservoir of the TM Field, a mature oil field in the South 
Sumatra Basin, Indonesia. Current oil saturation is estimated using a fractional-flow-based approach and validated 
by matching calculated and observed oil rates, resulting in Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE ) values of 
approximately 2%, 3%, and 3%. WOI represents reservoir opportunity through flow capacity, movable 
hydrocarbon pore volume, and remaining pressure support, while Delta Potential quantifies remaining production 
opportunity. The calculated Delta P otential values are 0.112, 0.103, and 0.042 MMSTB for TM -1, TM-2, and 
TM-3, respectively. Quadrant analysis places TM-1 and TM-2 in the high-WOI and high-Delta-Potential region; 
however, TM-2 is treated as a secondary candidate requiring further diagnosis because its WOI is lower than that 
of TM-1. The final weighted ranking produces the priority order TM -1 > TM-2 > TM-3. These results indicate 
that the proposed workflow provides a practical and traceable preliminary screening tool for mature -field 
production optimization under limited data availability.

1. Introduction 
Mature oil fields remain important contributors to hydrocarbon supply, but they commonly experience 
production decline, increasing water cut, reservoir pressure depletion, and decreasing well productivity. Mature -
field development studies have emphasized t hat remaining reserves, recovery factor, withdrawal rate, facility 
constraints, and reservoir maturity must be evaluated together to identify production improvement opportunities 
(Babadagli 2005; Johnson 2003; Sugiantoro 2014). Under these conditions, production optimization becomes 
increasingly dependent on the ability to identify wells that contain technically attractive remaining potential. In 
Indonesia, many mature fields show opportunities for production improvement, parti cularly when remaining 
reserves are relatively significant, but production withdrawal rates remain low (Sugiantoro 2014). Similar 
screening concepts have been applied in field redevelopment and enhanced recovery planning, where candidate 
prioritization considers remaining potential, operational feasibility, and spatial or facility constraints (Cimic 
2006; Romli et al., 2021) . This condition indicates that production optimization in mature fields should focus 
not only on increasing production rate, but also on improving the selection of wells or zones with the highest 
remaining opportunity.  
Candidate selection in mature fields is not straightforward because well performance is controlled by both 
reservoir condition and production behavior. A well may still have remaining recoverable oil, but its deliverability 
may be limited by low permeability, insufficient pressure support, high water cut, poor production efficiency, or 
data-quality limitations in water-production measurement (Chetri et al., 2008; Sandau 2008). Conversely, a well 
with favorable reservoir quality may not be an attractive optimization candidate if its remaining recoverable 
volume is limited. Therefore, candidate selection should not rely on a single production indicator or a single 
reservoir prop erty, but should instead integrate reservoir opportunity, remaining production potential, current 
production contribution, production efficiency, and the relative importance of the selected parameters (Haryanto 
et al., 2019; Pilcher et al., 2011). 
Several approaches have been developed to support production optimization and candidate recognition. 
Decline curve analysis is widely used to forecast production performance and estimate remaining recovery 
potential (Arps 1945). Production data-based methods have also been applied to identify underperforming wells 
and infill opportunities in mature fields, particularly under limited data availability (Jalali et al., 2006; Mattar & 
Anderson 2003 ; Mohaghegh et al., 2005) . Optimization and allocation studies further show that production 
improvement requires systematic integration of well -level performance and field -level constraints (Kosmidis et 
al., 2004) . The Heterogeneity Index and Modified Heterogeneity Index have been used to compare well 
performance, recognize production anomalies, and support candidate screening ( Reese 1996; Tan et al., 2010 ; 
Salman et al., 2013). In addition, opportunity-index concepts have also been used to support exploitation planning 
by ranking areas or wells according to their remaining potential (Molina & Rincon 2009) . Recent Indonesian 
studies have emphasized the importance of integrated workflows for matrix acidizing candidate selection, idle 
well reactivation, and bypassed oil identification by combining production, petrophysical, geological, and 
diagnostic data (Turnip et al., 2024; Larasati et al., 2026; Prayitno et al., 2026). 
Although these methods provide useful screening support, a practical gap remains in integrating reservoir -
based opportunity and production-based remaining potential into a traceable well-level decision workflow. Many 
mature fields lack complete static and dynamic reservoir models, making simulation-based optimization difficult 
to apply in early-stage screening. In such cases, engineers often rely on commonly available production history, 
petrophysical summaries, pressure data, and well performance indicators. A practical screening workflow is 
therefore necessary to combine these data sources into a consistent method for prioritizing well candidates before 
detailed intervention design, reservoir simulation, or economic evaluation. 
This study proposes an integrated well candidate selection workflow that combines Well Opportunity Index 
(WOI), Delta Potential, quadrant analysis, and weighted ranking. WOI represents reservoir-based opportunity by 
incorporating flow capacity, movable hydrocarbon pore volume, and remaining pressure support. Delta Potential 
represents production-based remaining opportunity by comparing estimated ultimate recovery with cumulative 
oil production. These two indicators are integrated through quadrant analysis t o classify candidate priority,

The objective of this study is to develop a practical and traceable workflow for selecting well candidates for 
production optimization in mature oil fields under limited data availability. The workflow is applied to three 
producing wells completed in the same reservoir interval of a mature oil field. The main contribution of this paper 
is the integration of reservoir opportunity and remaining production potential into a simple screening framework 
that can help reduce candidate misclassification and support early-stage production optimization decisions. 
 
2. Methodology  
This study develops a practical workflow for well candidate selection by integrating reservoir opportunity and 
remaining production potential. The workflow consists of six main stages: study data preparation, current oil 
saturation estimation, Well Opportunity Index calculation, Delta Potential calculation, quadrant-based candidate 
classification, and final weighted ranking. The proposed workflow is designed for mature fields where complete 
static and dynamic reservoir models are not available, but producti on history, petrophysical summaries, well 
pressure data, and completion information are accessible. Similar integrated screening philosophies have been 
used in production optimization, inactive-well audit, and well revitalization studies, where multiple indicators are 
combined to reduce uncertainty in candidate selection (Abdel-Basset et al., 2018; Haryanto et al., 2019; Turnip et 
al., 2024; Prayitno et al., 2026) . The final weighted ranking integrates normalized WOI and normalized Delta 
Potential using constant weights to determine the final well priority. 
2.1 Study data and workflow 
The workflow was applied to three producing wells, namely, TM-1, TM-2, and TM-3, completed in the same 
G-40 reservoir interval of the TM Field. The case study was conducted in the TM Field, a mature oil field in the

mainly associated with the Air Benakat Formation and is also supported by the Talang Akar Formation as part of 
the regional petroleum system. The field has reached a mature production stage, as indicated by production

2.4 Delta potential calculation 
Delta Potential was used to quantify the remaining production opportunity of each well. It represents the 
difference between estimated ultimate recovery and cumulative oil production. This study reviewed three EUR 
approaches: volumetric calculation, declin e curve analysis, and reciprocal analysis. Decline curve analysis 
extrapolates the production -rate decline trend, whereas reciprocal analysis estimates reserve from a reciprocal -
rate relationship. The EUR values used for Delta Potential were taken from the  volumetric trend, as this basis is 
more consistent with the reservoir -opportunity components used in WOI and reduces uncertainty caused by 
scattered production trends. Delta Potential (DP) was calculated using Equation 9,

where 𝐷𝑃, 𝐸𝑈𝑅 and 𝑁𝑝 are the remaining production potential, the estimated ultimate recovery, and  the

remaining production opportunity. However, Delta Potential alone does not guarantee that the well is technically

defined in this study to represent the remaining production opportunity as the difference between 𝐸𝑈𝑅 and 
cumulative oil production.

reservoir opportunity, while Delta Potential represents remaining production opportunity. This two -indicator

and production-potential ranking, where wells or areas are screened according to both reservoir quality and 
production response (Molina & Rincon 2009; Tan et al., 2010; Salman et al., 2013; Pilcher et al., 2011). The average

2.6 Weighted ranking 
After quadrant-based classification, a weighted ranking was applied to refine the final well priority. This step 
was included because quadrant analysis can identify the candidate category, but it does not fully quantify the 
relative priority among wells. Weighted and multi-criteria screening approaches have been applied in mature-field 
redevelopment, inactive-well audit, and production optimization workflows to improve transparency in candidate 
selection (Cimic 2006; Haryanto et al., 2019; Abdel-Basset et al., 2018). The weighted ranking in this study was 
developed using well potential parameters that represent both reservoir performance and production performance. 
In this study, the final ranking uses two normalized indicators: WOI as the reservoir -performance indicator 
and Delta Potential as the remaining -production-potential indicator. This revision avoids using the production -
maturity ratio Np/EUR as a positive contribution to the score, as a higher Np/EUR indicates a more depleted well 
and less remaining potential. For the base case, constant weights were assigned to all wells, with wI = 0.5 for 
normalized WOI and wD = 0.5 for normalized Delta Potential. Equal weights were selected because the proposed

behavior. This step is important because the movable hydrocarbon pore volume component of well opportunity 
index depends on the estimated current oil saturation. The saturation model was validated by matching calculated

for TM-1, 3% for TM-2, and 3% for TM-3. These values are below the 5% matching criterion used in this study,

3.4 Quadrant-based candidate classification 
WOI and Delta Potential were plotted in a two -dimensional screening space to integrate reservoir -based 
opportunity and remaining production opportunity. WOI was used as the reservoir -opportunity axis, while Delta 
Potential was used as the production-opportunity axis. 
Based on the combined WOI and Delta Potential evaluation, TM -1 is identified as the strongest primary 
candidate for production optimization. This well combines the highest WOI and the highest Delta Potential, 
indicating that it has both favorable reservoir conditions and the largest remaining production opportunity among 
the evaluated wells. 
TM-2 is classified as a secondary candidate. Although TM -2 has a Delta Potential value close to TM -1, its 
WOI is lower. This suggests that the remaining production opportunity in TM -2 may require further diagnostic 
evaluation to confirm whether it can be e ffectively produced. A possible follow -up evaluation may include a 
wellbore condition review, a production-system diagnosis, and an intervention feasibility assessment. 
TM-3 is classified as a lower -priority candidate because it has both the lowest WOI and the smallest Delta 
Potential. This result indicates that TM -3 has limited reservoir -based opportunity and limited remaining 
production potential compared with TM-1 and TM-2. 
The quadrant analysis demonstrates the value of integrating reservoir and production indicators. If candidate 
selection were based only on Delta Potential, TM-1 and TM-2 may appear similarly attractive. However, the WOI 
result shows that TM-1 has stronger reservoir support. Conversely, using WOI alone would not fully capture the 
remaining production opportunity. Therefore, combining both indicators reduces the risk of selecting wells based 
only on reservoir quality or remaining volume.

3.5 Weighted ranking results 
After quadrant -based classification, weighted ranking was applied to quantify the final well priority. The 
revised ranking used normalized WOI as the reservoir performance indicator and normalized Delta Potential as 
the remaining production potential indicator. Constant equal weights were applied to all wells in the base case. 
This formulation avoids assigning a positive score to the production-maturity ratio Np/EUR, as a higher Np/EUR 
indicates that a larger fraction of the estimated recovery has already been produced and therefore implies less 
remaining potential. 
 
 
The weighted ranking confirms that TM -1 is the highest -priority candidate, with a base -case final ranking 
factor of 1.00. In Table 7, DP denotes Delta Potential, while WOI* and DP* denote normalized values. TM -2 
ranks second with a final ranking factor of 0.88, while TM-3 ranks lowest with a final ranking factor of 0.40. The 
final priority order is therefore TM-1 > TM-2 > TM-3. 
This ranking is consistent with the quadrant -based screening. TM-1 is consistently identified as the strongest 
candidate because it combines the highest reservoir opportunity, the largest Delta Potential, and the highest final 
ranking factor. TM -2 remains a secondary candidate because it lies in the high -WOI and high -Delta-Potential 
region but has lower reservoir opportunity than TM -1. TM -3 is less attractive for short -term production 
optimization because both its reservoir and production-potential indicators are limited. 
A simple sensitivity analysis was performed by varying the relative importance of reservoir opportunity and 
remaining production potential. The base-case, reservoir-dominant, and production-dominant weighting scenarios 
all produced the same priority order: TM-1 > TM-2 > TM-3. 
The sensitivity results show that TM -2 has FRF values between 0.86 and 0.90, while TM -3 remains much 
lower, with FRF values between 0.39 and 0.41. This indicates that the ranking is robust for the evaluated three -
well case, although further calibration is needed for larger well populations and different reservoir settings. 
3.6 Discussion 
The results show that the proposed integrated workflow provides a more balanced framework for well 
candidate selection in mature fields. WOI represents reservoir -based opportunity by combining flow capacity, 
movable hydrocarbon pore volume, and remaining p ressure support. Delta Potential represents remaining 
production opportunity by comparing EUR and cumulative oil production. The weighted ranking then refines 
candidate priority by incorporating reservoir and production performance into a quantitative fina l score using 
constant and transparent weights. 
This integrated structure is important because mature -field candidate selection is often biased when only a 
single data type is used. Production -based indicators can identify wells with remaining volume or abnormal 
behavior, but they may not fully explain whether the reservoir has sufficient deliverability or pressure support. 
Reservoir-based indicators can identify wells with favorable subsurface properties, but they may not fully indicate 
whether the well has meaningful remaining recoverable potential. Th e proposed workflow bridges this gap by 
combining both perspectives in a sequential decision process.

The field case also demonstrates the practical value of the workflow under limited data conditions. The 
analysis was conducted using three wells in the same reservoir interval without a complete static or dynamic 
reservoir model. This condition is common i n mature-field evaluation, where engineers must often make early 
screening decisions using production history, petrophysical summaries, and pressure survey data. Compared with 
conventional HI or MHI approaches, which primarily evaluate relative production behavior or anomaly position, 
the proposed workflow adds reservoir opportunity through WOI and remaining production opportunity through 
Delta Potential. Compared with opportunity -index screening alone, the workflow also adds production -based 
remaining-potential evaluation and a final ranking step. Therefore, the method can serve as a preliminary 
screening tool before detailed well diagnosis, intervention design, reservoir simulation, or economic evaluation. 
The workflow should not be interpreted as a replacement for detailed reservoir and economic evaluation. 
The results are based on a limited number of wells within a single reservoir interval, and the weighting factors 
may need further calibration when appli ed to larger datasets or different reservoir settings. Sensitivity analysis 
using different WOI and Delta Potential weights did not change the ranking order in this case. For quadrant 
thresholds, the use of average values placed TM -1 and TM-2 in the high-WOI and high-Delta-Potential region, 
while a more conservative median -based threshold places TM -2 near the decision boundary. This supports the 
interpretation of TM -2 as a secondary candidate requiring further diagnosis. Nevertheless, the consistency 
among WOI, Delta Potential, quadrant classification, and weighted ranking indicates that the method can help 
reduce candidate -selection uncertainty and provide a transparent basis for prioritizing mature -field production 
optimization opportunities.  
 
 
 
 
The comparison with traditional screening approaches indicates that different single -parameter methods may 
lead to incomplete candidate interpretation. A production -only screening may identify wells with production 
anomalies, but it does not directly descr ibe reservoir support. A Delta-Potential-only approach makes TM-1 and 
TM-2 appear similarly attractive because both wells have relatively high remaining production opportunity. A 
WOI-only approach gives the order TM -1 > TM -2 > TM -3, but it does not explici tly quantify the remaining 
production volume. By integrating WOI and Delta Potential through weighted ranking, the proposed workflow 
preserves the same overall priority order while providing a clearer technical explanation: TM -1 is the primary 
candidate, TM-2 is a secondary candidate requiring further diagnosis, and TM-3 is a lower-priority candidate.

4. Conclusion 
This study developed a practical integrated workflow for well candidate selection in mature oil fields by 
combining Well Opportunity Index, Delta Potential, quadrant analysis, and weighted ranking. The workflow was 
designed for data-limited mature fields where complete static and dynamic reservoir models are not available, but 
production history, petrophysical summaries, pressure data, and completion information can be used to support 
early-stage candidate screening. 
The application to three wells in the G -40 reservoir interval showed that the fractional -flow-based current 
saturation estimation provided acceptable oil-rate matching results, with MAPE values of approximately 2% for 
TM-1, 3% for TM-2, and 3% for TM-3. These results indicate that production history can be used to infer current 
saturation behavior for screening purposes when direct saturation measurement is limited. 
The Well Opportunity Index calculation identified TM -1 as the well with the highest reservoir -based 
opportunity, with a WOI value of 147.19, followed by TM -2 with 123.38 and TM -3 with 62.08. Delta Potential 
analysis also showed that TM-1 had the largest remaining production opportunity at 0.112 MMSTB, followed by 
TM-2 at 0.103 MMSTB and TM-3 at 0.042 MMSTB. The combination of these two indicators through quadrant 
analysis consistently placed TM-1 as the primary candidate for production optimization. 
The final weighted ranking confirmed the priority order of TM-1 > TM-2 > TM-3, with base-case final ranking 
factors of 1.00, 0.88, and 0.40, respectively. Sensitivity analysis using reservoir -dominant and production -
dominant weighting scenarios did not change the priority order. This consistency between WOI, Delta Potential, 
quadrant classification, and weighted ranking demonstrates that the proposed workflow can provide a transparent 
and traceable basis for preliminary well candidate selection in mature fields. 
 Compared with traditional single -indicator screening, the proposed workflow provides a more complete 
interpretation of candidate priority. Delta Potential alone may place TM -1 and TM -2 as similarly attractive 
candidates, whereas WOI helps distinguish the stronger reservoir support of TM -1. The integrated ranking 
therefore reduces the risk of selecting a well based only on remaining production volume or reservoir quality. 
The workflow should be interpreted as a screening tool rather than a substitute for detailed reservoir 
simulation, well diagnosis, intervention design, or economic evaluation. Because this case study covers only three 
wells in a single reservoir interval, further application to larger well populations and different reservoir settings is 
recommended to calibrate the weighting factors, test alternative quadrant thresholds, and evaluate the robustness 
of the method under broader mature-field conditions.
