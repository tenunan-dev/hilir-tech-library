ABSTRACT - Recent exploration activities in Block “B” of the North Sumatra Basin, including the acquisition 
and processing of 610 km² 3D seismic data followed by a two-well exploratory drilling campaign, lead to gas and 
condensate discoveries within the Late Miocene sandstone reservoirs of the Lo wer Seurula Formation. The 
campaign targets two structures: Arun Overburden (AOB), located above the Arun Structure, and Rayeu. Previous 
studies focus primarily on the giant Arun and SLS Fields due to their substant ial hydrocarbon reserves. 
Consequently, direct evidence for the source rock charging reservoirs younger than the Early Miocene Peutu 
Formation carbonates remains limited. Gas and condensate samples from the newly discovered Rayeu CA and 
AOB-1 wells, along with samples from the Arun and SLS fields, are acquired and subsequently analyzed in the 
laboratory. Characterization and correlation of both source rock and hydrocarbon samples represent the most 
appropriate methodologies for evaluating their genetic relationship. Although condensates from Arun and AOB-
1 exhibit retrograde phase behavior, reliable geochemical interpretations and correlations are obtained. The results 
indicate that gas and condensate from both Peutu carbonate and Lower Seurula sandstone are  generated from a 
highly mature source rock dominated by algal organic matter, with only a minor contribution from higher plants. 
These findings suggest that multiple reservoir objectives in “B” Block, particularly in the Lhok Sukon Deep sub-
basin, are most consistently correlated with a mature Bampo-type marine shale source facies. 
Keywords: gas condensate, geochemical correlation, Lower Seurula Formation, Arun Field, North Sumatra Basin

1. Introduction 
The Arun and South Lhok Sukon (SLS) gas -condensate fields have been producing from the Early Miocene 
Peutu Formation carbonates since 1977 and 1997, respectively. After more than four decades of production, 
the recovery factor has reached 94% for the Arun Field and approximately 90% for the two fields combined. 
Despite their significant success, no major discoveries have been made in Block “B” to replace the reserves 
produced. Extensive exploration activities have been conducted by PT Pema Global Energi (PG E), the operator 
of the block since May 2021, including the acquisition and processing of 610 km 2 of 3D seismic data using 
vibroseis as the seismic source. This was followed by the drilling of two exploration wells, AOB -1 and Rayeu 
CA, which successfully tested gas and condensate within the Late Miocene Lower Seurula Formation sandstone 
reservoir inte rvals. 
Previous studies have primarily focused on the giant Arun and South Lhok Sukon (SLS) Fields because of their 
substantial hydrocarbon reserves. Consequently, comprehensive petroleum system studies of stratigraphic  
intervals younger than the Peutu Formation remain limited. The petroleum system of the North Sumatra  Basin, 
particularly within the North Lhok Sukon Deep sub-basin located near the Arun Field, has undergone significant 
reinterpretation and evolution over time. Early studies suggested that the gas and condensate accumulated within 
the Peutu Formation carbonate reservoirs of the Arun Field were sourced from shale intervals within the carbonate 
succession itself (Graves & Weegar 1973). Following revisions to the basin stratigraphic framework and 
nomenclature, source rocks for pre -Baong Formation reservoirs were interpreted to include shales of the Peutu, 
Belumai, and Bampo Formations (Kamili et al., 1976). 
Subsequent geochemical analyses of well cuttings identified the Baong and Bampo Formations as significant 
source rock candidates. Although the Baong Formation rarely contains more than 15 wt.% total organic carbon 
(TOC), its thickness was considered sufficient to contribute significantly to hydrocarbon generation (Davies 
1984). Using the 30 -Norhopane biomarker series, Subroto et al. (1992)  proposed that the oils within the Peutu 
Formation carbonates were derived from two potential source rocks: a mature carbonate-rich source facies and the 
shales of the Keutapang Formation and upper part of Baong Formation, although the maturity of these source 
intervals remained uncertain. Based on oil–source rock correlations using gas chromatographic and carbon isotope 
data, Buck and McCulloh (1994) proposed the Bampo–Peutu Petroleum System as the dominant active petroleum 
system in the North Sumatra Basin. In this model, the shale and marl of the Bampo  Formation act as the primary 
source rock, while the Peutu Formation serves as both a secondary source rock and a reservoir. Within this 
petroleum system, the Bampo, Peutu, and Baong Formations also function as regional seals. They are overlain by 
the Keutapang, Seurula, and Julu Rayeu Formations, together with Quaternary deposits that constitute the basin 
overburden. Geochemical studies of condensates indicate that the active petroleum system is primarily associated 
with lacustrine source rocks containing a marine algal contribution. However, carbonate source rocks may also 
be present (ten Haven & Schiefelbein 1995). Fuse et al. (1996)  identified the North Lhok Sukon Deep, located 
offshore northern Aceh, as a major hydrocarbon kitchen area, with the Bampo Formation shale acting as a Type 
II to Type II/III kerogen source rock. 
Lacustrine facies of syn -rift sediment from the Parapat or Bruksah Formation were postulated as potential  
source rock based on condensate analysis done (Tambupolon et al., 2018). More recently, Ariyono et al. (2021) 
suggested that shallow oil accumulations in the Bireuen and East Aceh areas originated from offshore fluvio -
lacustrine source rocks. In the eastern onshore area of the North Sumatra Basin, the Baong Formation has not 
reached maturity for hydrocarbon generation; hence, it does not cause overpressure in the formation                            
(Sijabat et al., 2023). 
No direct oil–source rock or gas–source rock correlation has been established for hydrocarbons accumulated 
within the Lower Seurula Formation sandstones in the North Sumatra Basin, particularly in the North Lhok Sukon 
Deep sub-basin. Therefore, this study aims to determine the principal source rock responsible for charging the 
Lower Seurula reservoirs. Based on previous studies, three hypotheses are evaluated: (1) predominant charging 
from the shale of the Bampo Formation; (2) contribution from the Baong Formation or other source intervals; and 
(3) mixed-source possibility or multi-phase charging throughout the history of the petroleum system of the basin.

This study provides new insights into the petroleum system of the North Sumatra Basin, particularly in the 
Lhok Sukon Deep sub-basin. It helps establish a refined exploration concept for the basin. The application of this 
concept is expected to reduce exploration uncertainty, improve the probability of discovery, and enhance 
operational efficiency, thereby strengthening the economic viability of future exploration campaigns in the “B” 
Block. More broadly, the results contribute to a better understanding of hydrocarbon generation and migration 
within the basin and may stimulate further oil and gas exploration throughout the North Sumatra Basin. 
 
2. Methodology 
The primary hydrocarbon dataset utilized in this study consists of one bottom-hole gas sample from the Rayeu 
CA well and five condensate samples collected from the Rayeu CA and AOB -1 discovery wells, together with 
one condensate sample each from the Arun a nd SLS Fields (Table 1) . These samples were analyzed by the 
Geological and Laboratory Services Division of PT Geoservices for gas composition, gas isotop composition, 
liquid and gas chromatography, GC -MS (Saturate and Aromatic Fractions), stable carbon isotope analysis 
(Saturate and Aromatic Fractions), and pressure –volume–temperature (PVT) properties. To support the 
interpretation, three PVT analyses of gas samples from the Arun and Arun A -1 wells conducted by Core 
Laboratories were also incorporated. Geochemical data from Arun wells, including TOC, Rock -Eval pyrolysis, 
organic petrography and GC, were obtained from Integrated Data Services (208 cuttings and 4 core samples), 
Sucofindo (108 cuttings samples), and Robertson Research (1 core sample) (Table 2). 
Gas and condensate characterization and correlation based on geochemical data constitute the primary 
methodology of this study. Hydrocarbon samples were subjected to molecular sieve isolation to improve 
biomarker, compositional, and stable isotope analyses. Source rock evaluation was conducted using 366 samples 
collected from seven wells in the Arun Field. Prior to analysis, soxhlet flushing was performed to extract the 
bitumen from any contaminants. The carbon isotopic composition plot from Buck and McCulloh (1994) and the 
sterane ternary diagram from Ariyono et al. (2021) were digitized and integrated into the analysis. In addition, the 
gas chromatogram of a Bampo Formation source rock extract from Buck and McCulloh (1994)  is utilized for 
source rock-hydrocarbon correlation. 
Geochemical logs were generated for each well to characterize potential source rocks with respect to organic 
matter quantity, quality, and thermal maturity. The interpretation of source rock parameters follows the 
guidelines of Merrill (1991) and Peters an d Cassa (1994). Gas composition (C1, C2, C3, iC4 and nC4) and 
PVT data were utilized to classify the gas and condensate types, including gas wetness and molecular ratio, as

Biomarker and carbon isotope parameters described by Peters et al. (2005) were applied to characterize the

2.1 Regional geology 
The North Sumatra Basin comprises a series of structural highs and depocenters. From west to east, these  
include the Rayeu Hinge Zone, Lhok Sukon Deep, Lhok Sukon High, Arun High, Jawa Deep, Bireuen High, and 
Samalanga Deep (Figure 1). The basin developed as a back-arc basin due to tectonic interaction between the Indo-

Australian and Eurasian plates from the Late Cretaceous to the Early Tertiary. The earliest stage  of basin 
development occurred during the Late Eocene pre-rift phase, characterized by the deposition of the calcareous 
shales of the Meucampli Formation and the limestones and dolomites of the Tampur Formation. Rifting 
commenced during the Late Eocene and was marked by the development of predominantly north–south-trending 
horst and graben systems that controlled the initial basin architecture and sedimentation patterns. Erosion of the 
uplifted horsts supplied sediments to the adjacent grabens, forming localized rift valleys. Th ese grabens were 
subsequently filled with syn -rift alluvial to fluvial deposits of the Bruksah Formation, representing the earliest 
clastic sediments deposited during basin evolution. 
The Late Oligocene –Early Miocene sag phase was characterized by regional subsidence and a progressive 
increase in water depth, leading to the inundation of the earlier rift topography. Anoxic to dysoxic depositional 
conditions facilitated the accumulation of the organic-rich shales of the Bampo Formation, marking the initiation 
of post -rift sedimentation. Continued marine transgression during the Early  to Middle Miocene promoted 
extensive carbonate platform development of the Peutu Formation over structural highs. Deposition of the Baong 
Formation shale during the Middle Miocene maximum flooding event subsequently terminated carbonate growth 
and provided an effective regional seal. Together, the Bampo, Peutu, and Baong Formations constitute the key 
source, reservoir, and seal elements of the North Sumatra Basin petroleum system. 
Middle Miocene wrench tectonism marked the transition from the post -rift to the regressive phase of basin  
evolution and was accompanied by uplift of the Barisan Mountain Range. This tectonic reorganization promoted the 
deposition of  the upper Baong Formation and the subsequent accumulation of fluvio -deltaic sediments of the 
Keutapang and Seurula Formations. Continued compressional deformation and associated volcanism during the 
Pliocene–Pleistocene reactivated earlier extensional faults, resulting in structural inversion  and the development 
of numerous hydrocarbon traps. These tectonic processes were fundamental to the migration, entrapment, and 
preservation of hydrocarbons within the North Sumatra Basin.

3. Result and Discussion 
The majority of source rock data were derived from wells in the Arun Field, located on the Arun High. Among 
the wells evaluated, only East Arun A-1 was drilled within the Lhok Sukon Deep (Figure 3).

3.1 Source rock determination 
Geochemical logs were constructed using data from four wells: Arun A -1, Arun A -7, Arun A -13, and East 
Arun A-1 (Figure 3). The discovery well of the Arun Field, Arun A -1, provided 13 cutting samples for analysis. 
Among these, only one black-shale sample from the Bampo Formation exhibited good source rock potential, with 
a TOC of 2.16 wt.% and a Tmax value of 480°C. Most samples from Arun A -7 were obtained from the Baong 
Formation shale and exhibited poor source rock quality, with all TOC values below 0.5 wt.%. Rock-Eval pyrolysis 
(REP) results from extracted samples of Arun A -13 suggest source rock potential  within the lower Keutapang 
Formation, Baong Formation, and Bampo Formation intervals. East  Arun A-1 provided 108 cutting samples 
spanning the Upper Seurula Formation at approximately 5,000 ft MD to the Bruksah Formation at 15,700 ft MD. 
Unlike the other wells, East Arun A-1 is located within the Lhok Sukon Deep and offers valuable insight into the 
deeper portion of the basin. Although several stratigraphic intervals exhibit source rock  potential, geochemical 
data indicate that source rocks encountered in the Arun High are generally immature. This interpretation is 
supported by both vitrinite reflectance measurements and Rock -Eval Tmax values, which suggest insuff icient 
thermal maturity for significant hydrocarbon generation.

The geochemical analyses conducted on samples from East Arun A -1 are considerably more comprehensive 
than those performed on the other wells. In addition to total organic carbon (TOC) measurements, Rock -Eval 
pyrolysis (REP), and organic petrographic analyses, gas chromatography was performed  (Figure 4). 
Quantitatively, source rock intervals from the Keutapang Formation down to the Bruksah Formation exhibit fair 
to good source rock potential. Based on the vitrinite reflectance trend across the entire stratigraphic section, thermal 
maturity reaches the onset of the oil -generation window (approximately 0.6 %Ro) within the lower part of the 
Baong Formation. This observation is significant because East Arun A -1 is the only well in the dat aset located 
within the Lhok Sukon Deep sub -basin. The Van  Krevelen diagram indicates that the potential source rock 
intervals are dominated by Type II and Type II/III kerogen (Figure 5), suggesting a predominantly oil - and gas-
prone organic matter assemblage. Combined with the observed maturity trend, these results demonstrate that the 
deeper stratigraphic succession in the Lhok Sukon Deep possesses favorable source rock characteristics and has 
begun to enter the hydrocarbon generation window. Gas chromatograms of rock extracts from Baong and Bampo 
Formations in the East Arun A-1 wells show peak n-alkane distributions between nC17-nC19. Corresponding 
Pristane/Phytane and Pristane/nC 17 ratios indicate the predominance of organic matter deposited under anoxic 
marine conditions, with a minor contribution from higher terrestrial plant material (Figure 6). 
3.2 Gas characterization 
Only one gas sample was collected from a bottom-hole sample in the Rayeu CA well, after a mechanical problem 
was encountered during the drilling of the AOB-1 well. The sample was submitted for gas composition and stable 
carbon isotope analyses. The gas contains a high proportion of hydrocarbons (84.43 mol.%), dominated by methane 
(70.17 mol.%). Its relatively high gas wetness value (14.9%) indicates that it is a wet gas (Table 3) . A low 
C1/(C2+C3) molecular ratio further suggests a thermogenic origin. In addition,  the absence of hydrogen sulfide 
classifies the gas as a sweet natural gas. The stable carbon isotope composition shows an approximately 20‰ 
difference between methane and ethane ( δ¹³C₂ − δ¹³C₁), suggesting a minor contribution of biogenic methane 
mixed with predominantly thermogenic gas (Figure 6). Furthermore, the gas maturity was evaluated using the co-
genetic ethane–propane relationship proposed by Faber (in Whiticar 1994). Application of this method suggests 
that the gas within the Lower Seurula sandstone  reservoir from the Rayeu CA well was generated from a highly 
mature source rock at an advanced stage of thermal maturity and is predominantly thermogenic, corresponding to 
an equivalent vitrinite reflectance of approximately 2.0 %Ro.

3.3 Condensate characterization 
An internal Arun Field fluid model report (1991) classified the Arun reservoir fluid as a retrograde condensate 
based on constant-volume depletion (CVD) tests performed on PVT samples from Arun A -1 and the Cluster II 
Production Facility (Figure 8). Two samples from Arun A -1 were analyzed at different reservoir depths. The 
shallower sample (9,953–9,958 ft MD) exhibited a retrograde dew-point pressure of 4,426 psig at 348°F, whereas 
the deeper sample (10,410 –10,415 ft MD) showed a dew -point pressure of 4,348  psig at 368°F. Both samples 
were obtained from a reservoir with a static pressure of 7,085 psig, and the maximum liquid dropout observed 
during depletion was approximately 0.7% of the original fluid volume. A surface sample collected from the

Cluster II Production Facility also displayed retrograde condensate behavior, with a dew-point pressure of 4,411 
psig at 352°F and a maximum liquid dropout of 1.13% of the original fluid volume. Similar results were obtained 
from two AOB-1 reservoir fluid samples, designated Zone 1 and Zone 2. The Zone 1 sample exhibited a dew -
point pressure of 3,044 psig at 224°F and a maximum liquid dropout of 14.1%, whereas the deeper Zone 2 sample 
showed a dew-point pressure of 4,180 psig at 225°F and a maximum liquid dropout of 15.8%. The occurrence of 
retrograde condensates has important implications for this study. On the one hand, these fluids retain sufficient 
biomarker compounds  to support reliable geochemical characterization and correlation. On the other hand, 
preferential partitioning  of lighter hydrocarbons into  the gas phase alters  the distribution  of n-alkanes and 
isoprenoids, affecting parameters such as the carbon preference index (CPI) and absolute chromatographic peak 
abundances. Retrograde condensates are commonly associated with hydrocarbons generated from Type II or Type 
II/III kerogen at advanced thermal maturity.

3.4 Gas-condensate to source rock correlation 
The condensate samples from the Arun Field, AOB-1, and Rayeu CA wells exhibit a unimodal n-alkane 
distribution characterized by the predominance of nC₁₅, nC₁₇, and nC₁₉ over nC₂₇, nC₂₉, and nC₃₁, together with 
relatively low abundances of odd-carbon-numbered homologs in the nC₂₃–nC₃₁ range. This distribution indicates 
a dominant contribution from marine organic matter, with only a minor input from terrestrial sources. Although 
the n-alkane envelope patterns of the Baong and Bampo Formation extracts are broadly similar, they differ in the 
position of their dominant peaks: the Baong Formation extract peaks at nC₁₉, whereas the Bampo Formation 
extract peaks at nC₁₇. Furthermore, the gradual decline in the abundance  of nC₂₀+ hydrocarbons observed in the 
condensate samples closely resembles the corresponding distribution pattern in extracts  from the Bampo 
Formation, suggesting a stronger genetic affinity with this source rock (Figure 10). 
Compared with the condensate samples, the  source rock extracts contain  a greater proportion of lighter 
hydrocarbons and generally exhibit lower Pr/Ph ratios (Figure 14). These differences, including a lower source 
rock maturity parameter of 0.6 %Ro from East Arun A -1 compared to the condensate and gas maturity levels, 
indicate that the condensates are thermally more mature than the analyzed source rock extracts and, therefore, are 
unlikely to have been generated locally from the analyzed intervals. Instead, the hydrocarbons were most likely 
expelled from a more deeply buried and thermally mature source kitchen (Figures 3 and 15). This interpretation 
suggests that the principal hydrocarbon kitchen is in the deeper part of the basin, north of the present-day Aceh 
coastline, within the Lhok Sukon Deep sub-basin. Hydrocarbons generated from the Bampo Formation shale are 
interpreted to have migrated vertically through the inverted bounding faults of the Lhok Sukon Deep and associated 
propagating fault systems. Subsequent lateral migration occurred along permeable Lower Seurula sandstone carrier 
beds, resulting in hydrocarbon charge and accumulation within the Lower Seurula reservoirs. 
Overall, the condensate and source rock samples appear to have been derived from a source rock that contains 
predominantly marine organic matter with a minor terrestrial contribution. The Arun condensate sample is 
isotopically slightly heavier than its nearest Bampo Formation extract sample, with a difference of less than 
1‰. In contrast, the isotopic difference between the Arun condensate and Baong Formation extract samples 
ranges from approximately 2‰ to 4‰. Similarly, the AOB -1 condensate sample is isoto pically slightly heavier 
than the closest Bampo Formation extract sample, with a difference of less than 1‰. In contrast, its isotopic 
separation from the Baong Formation extract samples exceeds 1‰. The Rayeu CA condensate sample is 
isotopically slightly h eavier than both the Baong and Bampo Formation extract populations. However, the 
isotopic composition of the saturated hydrocarbon fraction shows the closest affinity with the Bampo Formation 
extracts rather than those from the Baong Formation (Figure 16) . These isotopic relationships indicate a 
stronger genetic correlation between the condensates and the Bampo Formation source rock extracts. The 
consistently small isotopic differences observed between the Arun, AOB -1, Rayeu CA condensates, and the 
Bampo Formation extracts support the interpretation that the Late Oligocene Bampo Formation shale represents 
the principal source rock for hydrocarbons accumulated in the Arun, SLS, AOB, and Rayeu structures. 
Conversely, the larger isotopic offsets relative to th e Baong Formation extracts suggest that the Baong 
Formation played a less significant role in charging these accumulations.

4. Conclusion 
The North Sumatra Basin, particularly the Lhok Sukon Deep sub-basin, contains several potential source rock 
intervals; however, geochemical evaluation of source rock richness, kerogen quality, and thermal maturity suggests 
that the Baong, Peutu, Bampo, and Bruksah Formations constitute the most viable source rock candidates. Gas and 
condensate samples indicate generation from a highly mature petroleum system. The retrograde nature of the 
condensates further suggests derivation from Type II to Type II/III kerogen at advanced stages of thermal maturity. 
This confirms that an effective petroleum system was capable of charging younger reservoirs. 
Geochemical parameters, including biomarker distributions and Pr/Ph ratios, indicate that the source organic 
matter was deposited predominantly in a marine setting under anoxic to suboxic conditions, with only a minor 
terrestrial contribution. The biomarker signatures also support a shale-dominated source facies. 
Carbon isotope data demonstrate that condensates from the Rayeu CA and AOB-1 wells are genetically related 
to the Bampo Formation shale source rock. Condensates from the Arun Field exhibit similar isotopic and 
molecular signatures, suggesting a common sour ce origin. This interpretation is further supported by the close 
similarity between the n-alkane distributions of the condensates and those of extracts from the Bampo Formation. 
Consequently, gas and condensate samples from the Lower Seurula sandstone rese rvoir are most consistent with 
a highly mature Late Oligocene Bampo -type marine shale source facies. However, hydrocarbon migration from 
other pre-Baong potential source rock intervals could not be entirely ruled out. 
The recent gas and condensate discoveries within the Lower Seurula Formation sandstone reservoirs provide 
compelling evidence for an active petroleum system in the Lhok Sukon Deep Sub -basin, North Sumatra Basin. 
The results indicate that hydrocarbons gener ated from the Bampo Formation marine shale source rock have 
migrated into both the Peutu carbonate reservoirs and the stratigraphically younger Lower Seurula sandstone 
reservoirs. This migration is interpreted to have occurred through a combination of faul t-related pathways and 
permeable carrier beds, enabling vertical and lateral hydrocarbon charge across multiple stratigraphic intervals.
