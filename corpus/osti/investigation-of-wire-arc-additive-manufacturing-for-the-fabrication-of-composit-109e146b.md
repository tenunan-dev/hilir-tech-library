# Investigation of wire arc additive manufacturing for the fabrication of composite autoclave molds

**Authors:** Sebok, Michael [ORNL] (ORCID:000000020655195X); Lamsey, Matthew [University of Tennessee, Knoxville (UTK)]; McNeil, Logan [University of Tennessee, Knoxville (UTK)]; Hamel, William R. [University of Tennessee, Knoxville (UTK)]; Nycz, Andrzej [ORNL] (ORCID:0000000174156559); Yamamoto, Yukinori [ORNL] (ORCID:0000000345744890); acevedo, Obed Daniel [University of Tennessee, Knoxville (UTK)]; Noakes, Mark [ORNL] (ORCID:0000000300050959); Patrick, Steven [ORNL] (ORCID:0000000225615998); Meyer, Luke [ORNL] (ORCID:0000000242172076); Hill, Leon [GKN Aerospace]; Allison, Christopher [GKN Aerospace]; Dodge, Amanda [Lincoln Electric Company]; Barnhart, Bradley [Lincoln Electric Company]; Hassen, Ahmed [ORNL] (ORCID:0000000328521222)

**Abstract:** Current manufacturing techniques for Invar composite autoclave molds are manual labor-intensive, and result in long lead times. With the emergent application of wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) to the manufacturing of composite molds, lead times can be shortened considerably. In this work, a design and fabrication framework for incorporating WAAM into the manufacturing of composite molds for aerospace applications is explored. Tensile testing and coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) analysis confirmed that WAAM deposition does not substantially alter the material properties most critical to the performance of Invar molds. A demonstrator mold geometry was refined and validated within simulations, manufactured in a continuous process using WAAM, and machined to specification with a final subtractive manufacturing step. Initially, the demonstrator geometry was manufactured from low-alloy steel, and this process informed the manufacture of the final Invar demonstrator. The Invar demonstrator was completed approximately 80% faster and with 40% less material when compared against manufacturing of the conventional mold design. The results address the need for an advanced manufacturing workflow which streamlines the production of critical Invar aerospace tooling. The constructed parts demonstrate the value of a workflow with WAAM integration for composite mold construction and its potential to transform legacy manufacturing processes.
