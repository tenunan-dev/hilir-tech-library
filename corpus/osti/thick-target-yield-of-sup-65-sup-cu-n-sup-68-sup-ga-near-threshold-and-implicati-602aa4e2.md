# Thick-target yield of <sup>65</sup>Cu(α,n)<sup>68</sup>Ga near threshold and implications for nuclear medicine, deep underground detector backgrounds, and nucleosynthesis

**Authors:** Meisel, Zachary [Air Force Institute of Technology, Wright-Patterson AFB, OH (United States)] (ORCID:0000000284038879); Hamad, G. [University of Virginia School of Medicine, Charlottesville, VA (United States)] (ORCID:0009000980541483); Brandenburg, K. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)]; Brune, C. R. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)] (ORCID:0000000336963311); Carter, D. E. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)]; Derkin, J. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)] (ORCID:0000000300145923); Jones-Alberty, Y. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)] (ORCID:0000000201312204); Massey, T. N. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)] (ORCID:0000000255785357); Soltesz, D. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)]; Subedi, S. K. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)] (ORCID:0000000158981989); Voinov, A. V. [Ohio University, Athens, OH (United States)]

**Abstract:** We report thick target yields of $^{65}{\rm Cu}(\alpha,n)^{68}{\rm Ga}$ from $\alpha$ energies $E_{\alpha}$$=$6.5--8~MeV measured via neutron-counting at the Edwards Accelerator Laboratory at Ohio University. Our results agree with prior results based on single-foil irradiations, while disagreeing with thick-target yields inferred from stacked-foil irradiations by up to an order of magnitude. Based on comparisons of our and prior measurements to Hauser-Feshbach calculations, we estimate the thick-target yield for $^{68}{\rm Ga}$ from $E_{\alpha}$=14 MeV impinging on 100%-enriched copper ($^{65}{\rm Cu}$) is 161$^{+5}_{-24}$ MBq/$\mu$Ah (for He$^{++}$), which is in agreement with the current IAEA estimate of 156 MBq/$\mu$Ah. At $E_{\alpha}$$=$8 MeV, where radioimpurity-free production of $^{68}{\rm Ga}$ could be realized with a natural copper target, our yield estimate is nearly 4$\times$ lower than the IAEA estimate. Here, we find that the $^{65}{\rm Cu}(\alpha,n)$ cross section estimates presently employed for neutrino and dark matter detector backgrounds and the $^{65}{\rm Cu}(\alpha,n)$ astrophysical reaction rate recently recommended for nucleosynthesis calculations are in agreement with our measurement results.
