# Conceptual design of highly-constrained splitters for the FFA@CEBAF energy upgrade study

**Abstract:** Jefferson Lab is investigating a significant energy upgrade to the Continuous Electron Beam Accelerator Facility (CEBAF) utilizing Fixed-Field Alternating-gradient (FFA) recirculating arcs. Here, the upgrade requires one horizontal beam splitter per recirculating arc, each receiving up to six collinear beam passes spanning 10.55 to 21.55 GeV, separating them into independent beamlines, and delivering each pass with individually matched optics into the permanent-magnet FFA arcs. This is the first splitter design for a non-ring FFA system at GeV-scale energies. The design challenge is severe: a single conventional-magnet system must simultaneously satisfy independent Twiss parameter (α, β), dispersion (η, η′), time-of-flight, and momentum compaction (R<sub>56</sub>) requirements for all six passes, within the tight spatial envelope of the existing CEBAF tunnel, while absorbing all path-length correction functions currently distributed across CEBAF’s dogleg magnets. A nested chicane architecture enables path-length and R<sub>56</sub> management within the available transverse space, and dedicated per-pass matching sections provide the independent optics control required by the fixed-field arc design. Six viable matching solutions are demonstrated across two LINAC input optics conditions and two FFA arc match points, confirming the robustness and flexibility of this conceptual design as the broader FFA@CEBAF upgrade continues to evolve. An integrated high-energy beam extraction scheme is also presented.
