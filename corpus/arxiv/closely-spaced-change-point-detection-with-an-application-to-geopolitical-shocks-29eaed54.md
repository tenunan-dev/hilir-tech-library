# Closely spaced change-point detection with an application to geopolitical shocks in oil and gas markets

**Authors:** Nachiketas Waychal, Arnab Kumar Laha, Ankur Sinha

**Abstract:** Change points that arrive in quick succession, or near the boundaries of the sample, are prone to type-II error: the few observations trapped between successive breaks carry too little information for global procedures to analyze. This article develops a test statistic, $I_1$, for which we derive the limiting null distribution under Gaussian noise, extend the theory to weakly dependent sequences, and prove consistency under a mean-shift alternative. Around $I_1$, we construct DCI, a two-phase algorithm that couples a global scan with multiple local tests via an adaptive-width sliding window. In extensive simulations, $I_1$ and DCI remain Pareto-optimal over criteria spaces spanning true and false positive rates, localization error, and segmentation covering. An application to the US-Iran 2026 conflict quantifies the exposure of energy markets to geopolitical shocks using two new metrics, event sensitivity and event attribution, leading to time series tokenization.
